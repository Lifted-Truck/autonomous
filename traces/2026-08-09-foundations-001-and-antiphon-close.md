**report, not a request**, correctly applying the two-consumer rule: one
consumer of a pattern is not a promotion case.

The second consumer was this repo, 13 days earlier, unknown to them. Both
parties independently hit "a hand-maintained roster of relationships diverges
from observable reality, silently," and both landed on the same resolution
*including the same deliberate restraint* — detect and report, never mutate the
roster. Their words: "discovering a repo is not registering it." Mine, in

Convergent design under independent derivation is the strongest evidence the
two-consumer rule can produce. It only surfaced because FOUNDATIONS filed a
finding it had **already resolved locally**, for no reason other than not
wanting the resolution to become private divergence. That is the protocol
working upward — from an execution project into the standards it runs under —
which is the direction it is usually weakest.

Their four-state registry also exposed a gap in mine: I have no
`deferred-with-revisit-trigger` state, so a finding I have deliberately declined
re-surfaces every sweep and trains the reader to skip it.
